# What Is First-Hour Rating on a Water Heater?

A 50-gallon tank does not promise 50 gallons of useful hot water and then stop.

First-hour rating estimates how much hot water a fully heated storage water heater can deliver during a busy first hour. It combines the hot water already stored with the water heated while the tank recovers. Two tanks with the same gallon label can therefore deliver different first-hour performance.

If you are comparing storage water heaters, the gallon number tells you the container. First-hour rating tells you more about the job it can do.

## What the rating measures

Imagine the water heater begins fully heated. The household starts drawing hot water. Cold water enters the tank, and the burner, electric elements, or heat-pump system begins replacing heat.

First-hour rating represents the estimated hot-water delivery across that first hour under the applicable test procedure. It gives you a consistent product comparison. Conditions inside the home still determine the experience.

Actual experience can change with:

- Incoming-water temperature.
- Thermostat setting and delivered-water controls.
- Household draw pattern.
- Installation and equipment condition.
- Operating mode on a heat-pump water heater.
- Recirculation or distribution losses.
- A model’s recovery performance.

Use the rating to compare tested products. Use household and site information to decide whether the rating is enough.

## Why tank size alone can mislead you

Tank size is easy to see. Recovery is easy to overlook.

A heater with stronger recovery can replenish more hot water during the hour. Another heater may store more water and recover more slowly. Both can carry a familiar gallon label while serving a busy household differently.

This is the reason a 40-versus-50-gallon article cannot give every family a reliable answer. The decision needs the first-hour rating, the peak-hour demand, and the installation constraints.

Read the full [water-heater sizing guide](/resources/water-heater-sizing) before treating any one rating as the recommendation.

## Where to find first-hour rating

Look for first-hour rating in the manufacturer’s current specification, product documentation, or applicable consumer label for the exact model. Record the model number while comparing. Product families can contain units with different inputs, capacities, and performance.

Do not transfer a rating from a nearby model or a retailer listing without checking the manufacturer’s data.

If the unit is ENERGY STAR certified, use the current ENERGY STAR Product Finder as another verification point for eligible product data. Certification status and catalog entries can change, so record the checked date.

## Estimate the household’s peak hour

The useful comparison is first-hour rating against the household’s busiest realistic hour.

Write down the sequence and overlap:

- Showers and their approximate duration.
- Baths or large tub fills.
- Dishwasher or clothes-washer use during the same period.
- Handwashing, food preparation, and other meaningful draws.
- Any recirculation or unusual fixture demand.

Use measured or well-supported fixture information where possible. Do not create precision by adding generic gallons from an unsourced chart.

The estimate is a planning tool. A qualified professional should confirm the final model against the home, plumbing, and manufacturer data.

## How heat-pump modes affect the conversation

Heat-pump water heaters can use different operating modes. DOE guidance explains that these modes can trade efficiency, recovery speed, and resistance-element use.

The published first-hour performance gives you a comparison point. The selected mode and real use pattern still matter. A household that repeatedly forces high-demand or resistance operation may get the hot water it needs with different energy performance than the headline efficiency suggests.

Ask which mode and tank size the recommendation assumes. Then check the current manual for that exact model.

## What first-hour rating cannot tell you

First-hour rating does not answer every sizing or ownership question.

It cannot confirm:

- Whether the equipment fits the room.
- Whether gas, venting, electricity, airflow, or condensate requirements can be met.
- Whether the household’s peak estimate is accurate.
- Whether a current heater has a fault or changed performance.
- Whether a larger tub or future household change alters demand.
- Whether the product has qualified local service.

It also does not size a tankless water heater. Tankless selection relies on simultaneous hot-water flow and design temperature rise.

## First-hour rating and recovery belong together

If first-hour rating tells you what a fully heated storage system can deliver during the first busy hour, recovery helps explain what happens as the system replaces the used heat.
