# How to Compare Water-Heater Quotes

Two quotes can be $2,000 apart and still describe the same cost.

The lower bid may omit the permit, drain work, electrical circuit, venting, code corrections, disposal, startup, or labor warranty that appears in the higher bid. Put every quote into the same columns before comparing totals: exact model, performance, removal, installation, permit, corrections, conversion work, controls, warranty, service, and exclusions.

A complete scope makes the price comparable. It does not prove the installer is qualified.

## Start with the same job

If one installer priced a like-for-like tank replacement and another priced a heat-pump conversion, you do not have competing quotes. You have competing projects.

Write the intended outcome at the top of the comparison:

- Replace the existing system with the same technology and suitable capacity.
- Correct known installation issues.
- Increase hot-water performance.
- Convert to another technology.
- Add recirculation, leak protection, drainage, or relocation.

When two paths remain plausible, request a separate written scope for each. Do not let an equipment upgrade, code correction, and optional feature disappear inside one total.

## Compare the exact equipment

Every quote should name:

- Manufacturer and exact model number.
- Technology and fuel or power type.
- Tank volume and first-hour rating for storage systems.
- Flow at the relevant temperature rise for tankless systems.
- Efficiency rating and applicable certification.
- Included controls, recirculation components, service valves, pans, alarms, or accessories.
- Product, parts, tank or heat-exchanger, and labor warranty terms.

“50-gallon water heater” is not enough. Neither is “high-efficiency tankless.”

Use the manufacturer’s current documents to verify the model. If the quote claims an installer is authorized or certified by a manufacturer, verify that status in the manufacturer’s current directory for the company and location involved.

Water Heater Advisor does not treat a badge on a proposal as proof.

## Compare the performance assumption

Ask each installer to show why the proposed unit fits the household.

For storage systems, compare peak demand, tank volume, first-hour rating, recovery, and any heat-pump mode assumption. For tankless systems, compare simultaneous hot-water flow and the cold-end temperature rise.

The [water-heater sizing guide](/resources/water-heater-sizing) explains those measurements. A product can be installed correctly and still disappoint the household when it was sized from a head count alone.

## Compare removal and basic installation

Confirm whether the quote includes:

- Delivery and safe access to the location.
- Shutdown and disconnection of the old unit.
- Drain-down, removal, haul-away, and disposal.
- Placement and connection of the new unit.
- Required shutoffs, connectors, fittings, and support.
- Startup, commissioning, testing, cleanup, and homeowner orientation.
- Protection and repair of finished areas affected by the work.

Ask what “standard installation” excludes. Put the answer in writing.

## Compare permit, inspection, and corrections

The quote should state:

- Which authority has jurisdiction.
- Whether a permit and inspection are required for the stated work.
- Who obtains the permit and schedules inspection.
- The permit fee or allowance.
- Which known code corrections are included.
- Who pays when the inspector requires additional work outside the known scope.

Do not accept a national article or installer’s casual statement as the local code decision. Verify the authority and requirements for the address.

Use [local water-heater guidance](/local) where Water Heater Advisor has a verified evidence set.

## Compare water, gas, venting, electrical, air, and drain work

This is where technology conversions often separate.

### Water and damage protection

Identify piping changes, shutoffs, connectors, drain pan, drain route, leak detection, automatic shutoff, pressure or expansion-related work, and finish repair. The local requirements and installation decide which items apply.

### Gas and venting

For fuel-burning equipment, identify gas-capacity verification, line or regulator work where applicable, vent material and route, combustion-air requirements, termination, condensate on condensing equipment, and startup or combustion testing performed by qualified people.

Do not accept “gas line included” without a size, route, and scope.

### Electrical

Identify voltage, dedicated circuit, disconnect, wiring route, panel capacity, panel or service work, electrical permit, inspection, and who performs the work.

A heat-pump water-heater quote that says “electrical by others” is incomplete until the other price exists.

### Airflow and condensate

For a heat-pump water heater, identify the installation space, air-volume or ducting approach, clearances, noise considerations, condensate route, drain or pump, and any effect on adjacent rooms.

The exact manufacturer manual controls the equipment requirements.

## Compare recirculation and controls

Recirculation can improve wait time and change installation, controls, energy use, return-path requirements, and warranty considerations.

Ask whether the proposal includes recirculation, what type, how it is controlled, how it interacts with the proposed water heater, and what maintenance it adds. Do not compare one quote with recirculation to another without it as if the equipment totals describe the same system.

## Compare warranties and service after installation

Separate the warranties:

- Manufacturer tank or heat-exchanger coverage.
- Manufacturer parts coverage.
- Installer labor warranty.
- Warranty registration responsibility.
- Maintenance conditions.
- Diagnostic, travel, shipping, removal, and reinstall costs.

Then ask who services the product locally after the sale. A long manufacturer warranty has less practical value when no qualified company will diagnose the unit or stock the needed part.

Warranty length does not prove reliability. It describes the written coverage and exclusions.

## Compare allowances, exclusions, and change orders

Circle every allowance and exclusion.

Common examples include:

- Permit or inspection fee.
- Electrical or panel work.
- Gas meter, utility, or regulator work.
- Venting beyond a stated length.
- Condensate drain or pump.
- Drywall, roofing, painting, cabinetry, platform, or structural repair.
- Hazardous-material testing or removal.
- Code corrections discovered after opening work.
- Water treatment or recirculation.
- Temporary hot water or additional visits.

Ask how change orders are approved and priced before work begins. A bid with a low total and broad exclusions is a starting price.

## Verify the installer separately

The scope and the installer are two different checks.

Use the [water-heater installer guide](/installers/how-to-choose) to verify license status through the relevant regulator, insurance or bonding where applicable, experience with the exact technology, permit responsibility, local service, references or complaint context, and who actually performs each trade.

Water Heater Advisor is not a plumbing contractor and does not perform the work. It may be paid by installers for completed jobs. Payment may affect which eligible installer receives an introduction. It must never affect the system recommendation.

## Get the recommendation before choosing the quote

The lowest complete quote can still be for the wrong system.

A general checklist cannot know whether your household needs more first-hour delivery, whether tankless can meet the cold-end flow, whether a heat pump fits the room, or whether conversion work makes sense.
