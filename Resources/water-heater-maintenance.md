# Water Heater Maintenance Schedule

The manufacturer owns your warranty and sets the maintenance schedule.

Use the current manual for the exact model as the governing schedule. A useful general plan is to inspect the exterior regularly for leaks, corrosion, damage, blocked air paths, and changed performance; keep the area clear as instructed; retain service records; and arrange model-specific work at the stated interval. Tasks involving hot water, pressure, gas, combustion, electricity, refrigerant, or difficult access may require a licensed professional.

The goal is a maintenance record tied to the heater you own.

## Build the plan around the exact model

Start with the rating plate. Record:

- Manufacturer, model, and serial number.
- Installation date and installer, if known.
- Fuel or power type.
- Current manual and warranty links or saved copies.
- Required maintenance tasks and intervals.
- Water-quality conditions or treatment relevant to the warranty.
- Service company and qualified local support.
- Permit and installation records.

Manual revisions and product families matter. Use documentation for the exact model rather than a nearby unit with a similar name.

## Regular exterior observation

You can learn a great deal without opening the equipment.

At a regular interval that fits the installation, look for visible water, new staining, corrosion, physical damage, blocked airflow, stored items that violate clearance instructions, a full drain pan, changed noise, error indicators, or reduced hot-water performance.

EPA WaterSense recommends regular visual inspection of water heaters for leaks, aged gaskets or loose connections, and corrosion. The page also says professional service may be the better way to ensure the whole system is checked.

Observation is not permission to tighten, disassemble, reset, test, or repair. If you see an active leak or immediate hazard, use the [water-heater emergency guide](/emergency).

## The annual review conversation

“Annual maintenance” is often treated as one universal procedure. Actual tasks differ by technology, model, installation, water conditions, and manufacturer instructions.

Use an annual calendar reminder to review the manual and service record even when the manual sets another task interval. Ask:

- Is a qualified inspection due?
- Does the manual call for flushing, filter cleaning, anode inspection, vent or combustion inspection, condensate work, or another task?
- Did water conditions, household use, or equipment performance change?
- Is the warranty tied to documented service?
- Are any source documents, safety notices, or manufacturer instructions newer?

This annual review does not mean every component should be operated or removed by the homeowner once a year.

## Gas storage water-heater maintenance

Gas storage equipment adds combustion, venting, gas supply, and burner systems to the tank and water-side components.

Keep combustion-air and clearance areas as the manual requires. Have a qualified professional address burner, flame, gas, venting, combustion, and carbon-monoxide concerns. Do not use a generic checklist to relight equipment, alter venting, or diagnose combustion.

Tank-side questions can include sediment, anode condition, temperature controls, drain protection, and the temperature-and-pressure relief system. Each carries product and safety limits.

Read [how often a water heater should be flushed](/resources/how-often-flush-water-heater) and [what an anode rod does](/resources/water-heater-anode-rod) before assuming either task applies on a fixed schedule.

## Electric storage water-heater maintenance

Electric storage equipment removes combustion and venting from the job. It still contains high voltage, hot water, pressure, heating elements, controls, and a storage tank.

Do not remove access panels or test live electrical components from a general article. Exterior observation, records, water-condition review, and model-specific professional service remain the safe framework.

## Tankless water-heater maintenance

Tankless maintenance can include water-side scale management, filters or strainers, condensate systems on condensing models, venting, combustion, recirculation, and model-specific inspections.

Water conditions and manufacturer instructions change the schedule. “Flush every tankless heater once a year” is too broad to govern every model and home. The manual should identify the procedure, required solution or equipment, qualifications, and conditions that change service.

The existing [tankless guide](/water-heaters/tankless) explains why maintenance burden belongs in the purchase decision. The [hard-water guide](/resources/hard-water-water-heater) explains why a citywide assumption cannot replace measured water information.

## Heat-pump water-heater maintenance

An integrated heat-pump water heater combines a storage tank with a fan, air filter, refrigeration system, condensate, controls, and backup resistance elements.

DOE says filters require regular cleaning and directs owners to the model manual for the timing and method. Keep airflow and clearances consistent with the installation instructions. Have qualified service address refrigerant, electrical, condensate, fan, control, or tank concerns beyond the owner-safe manual steps.

Mode changes and a dirty filter can affect performance in ways that feel like a capacity issue. Record the mode and maintenance history before service.

## Water conditions change the maintenance question

Hardness, scale, sediment, corrosivity, treatment, and source changes do not mean the same thing.

Use a current utility water-quality report or a home-specific test where needed. Compare the result with the manufacturer’s requirements. Do not install treatment or change maintenance solely because an article labels an entire city “hard water.”

Read [how hard water affects a water heater](/resources/hard-water-water-heater).

## Keep a maintenance record

Create one record for the life of the unit:

| Date | Observation or service | Person/company | Source or manual section | Parts/materials | Result / next due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

Attach invoices, photos, measurements, water reports, warranty communications, and the manual version used. If the unit later needs repair or replacement, the record turns “I think it was maintained” into evidence.

## Know when maintenance becomes diagnosis

Reduced hot water, a recurring code, a leak, new corrosion, a changed sound, or repeated shutdown is no longer a calendar task. It is a performance or safety question.

Use the [failure warning-sign guide](/resources/water-heater-failure-warning-signs) and [running-out-of-hot-water guide](/resources/running-out-of-hot-water). Do not keep flushing, resetting, or adjusting equipment in the hope that a maintenance action will erase a fault.

## Plan the next system before this one fails

Maintenance can protect performance and reveal changes. It cannot make every current system the right future system.

If the heater no longer fits the household, use the maintenance record during the replacement decision.
