# How Often Should You Flush a Water Heater?

“Once a year” is useful only when the exact water heater and its manufacturer agree.

There is no reliable universal flushing interval. The right schedule depends on the model, water conditions, sediment or scale, installation, use, service history, and manufacturer instructions. EPA notes that annual flushing is commonly recommended, while also directing homeowners to the product instructions or a professional. Treat that as general context. Let the exact manual govern the job.

Hot water, pressure, electricity, gas, drain routing, and a valve that does not operate as expected can turn routine advice into water damage or injury.

## What flushing is meant to address

Storage water heaters can collect material at the bottom of the tank. The amount and type depend on the water supply, temperature, treatment, system design, and operation.

Tankless systems can develop mineral scale in water passages or heat exchangers under certain conditions. Their service procedure differs from draining sediment out of a storage tank.

Flushing may be part of managing these conditions. It does not repair a leaking tank, restore a damaged component, correct unsafe combustion, solve every odor, or guarantee a longer lifespan.

Start by naming the problem you expect the task to solve.

## Find the exact manual before choosing an interval

Record the manufacturer, model, serial number, installation date, and water-heater type. Retrieve the current manual and warranty for that exact model.

Look for:

- Whether flushing or descaling is required.
- The stated interval or condition that triggers service.
- Water-quality limits or different schedules.
- Required materials, equipment, and procedure.
- Warnings for an older or neglected unit.
- Whether qualified or licensed service is required.
- Warranty documentation requirements.

Do not borrow a procedure from another model because both units are gas tanks, tankless, or made by the same brand.

## Water conditions can change the schedule

Hardness can contribute to mineral scale. Sediment can enter or form under other water and system conditions. Corrosivity and water treatment create separate questions.

A city name does not measure the water reaching one home. Water sources can vary, and a utility report may describe a systemwide range rather than the exact tap.

Use a current utility water-quality report or a qualified test when water conditions affect service. Then compare the evidence with the manufacturer’s instructions.

Read [does hard water damage a water heater?](/resources/hard-water-water-heater) before treating hardness, sediment, corrosion, and safety as one issue.

## Storage and tankless flushing are different jobs

### Storage water heaters

The task can involve high-temperature water, a pressurized vessel, a drain valve, power or fuel controls, and a discharge route capable of handling the water safely. Older equipment or a valve that has not been operated can add risk.

EPA WaterSense gives general homeowner maintenance guidance, including shutting off the power supply and directing hot discharge to a suitable location. That general summary does not replace the exact manual, site conditions, or professional judgment.

This article intentionally does not provide a universal step-by-step procedure.

### Tankless water heaters

Tankless descaling or flushing can involve service valves, pumps, solutions, filters, condensate, gas or electrical controls, and model-specific sequences. The manufacturer controls the approved method and materials.

If the installation lacks the expected service access, the unit has an error, or the procedure crosses into gas, combustion, electrical, or permit-controlled work, use a qualified professional.

## Signs that call for diagnosis before flushing

Do not use flushing as a guess when the heater shows:

- An active leak.
- A recurring error or shutdown.
- A sudden loss of hot-water output.
- Dangerous temperature behavior.
- Combustion, gas, or carbon-monoxide concern.
- Wet electrical equipment.
- Severe corrosion or damaged components.
- A drain valve or connection that appears unsafe to operate.

Use the [water-heater warning-sign guide](/resources/water-heater-failure-warning-signs) or [emergency guide](/emergency) first.

## What if the heater has never been flushed?

An older system with no service history deserves model-specific judgment before someone opens a drain path and assumes it will close again.

Tell the service professional the age, model, installation, visible condition, water source, treatment, and known maintenance history. Ask what the manufacturer says, what risk the current condition creates, and what the service can reasonably accomplish.

An honest answer may be “inspect before deciding.”

## Record the service and result

After qualified flushing or descaling, record:

- Date and person or company performing the work.
- Manual and procedure used.
- Water conditions or reason for the interval.
- Materials used where relevant.
- Observed result and any limitation.
- Next review or service date.
- Warranty documentation.

Add the record to the [water-heater maintenance schedule](/resources/water-heater-maintenance). If performance does not improve, return to diagnosis rather than repeating the procedure.

## The next step

Use the exact manual first. If the instructions, age, access, water temperature, pressure, fuel, power, drain route, or equipment condition create doubt, use a licensed professional who services that model.

For a system that is repeatedly scaling, losing output, or becoming expensive to maintain, the purchase question may need to reopen. The [repair-or-replace guide](/resources/repair-or-replace-water-heater) shows how to make that decision without turning one maintenance event into an automatic replacement.
