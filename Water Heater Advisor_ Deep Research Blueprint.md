# **Water Heater Advisor: Deep Research Blueprint**

**Research date: August 6, 2026**

Your core positioning is sound. Water Heater Advisor should remain an independent education and matching platform, not present itself as a plumbing contractor. The recommendation should come first, then the installer connection. That matches the original business model in your brief.

## **1\. Executive summary**

**Recommendation: proceed with the Modesto MVP. Do not build the nationwide site yet.**

The opportunity is stronger than a standard plumbing lead-gen site for four reasons:

1. **Water heaters create complex decisions.** Homeowners need to choose between repair, replacement, tankless, storage, heat pump, gas, electric, brands, sizes, installers, rebates, and upgrades.  
2. **Most current information comes from someone selling something.** Manufacturers favor their products. Contractors favor what they install. Retailers want the equipment sale. Lead marketplaces want the homeowner's contact information.  
3. **Different leads have different value to different contractors.** A Navien conversion lead, failed 40-gallon tank, HPWH prospect, repair call, and annual tankless flush should not all go to the same buyer.  
4. **Local conditions materially change the recommendation.** Utility rates, rebates, electrical infrastructure, permits, climate, local contractor expertise, and even water quality can change the answer.

That creates a real role for an independent advisor.

The defensible business is not:

> "Water heater SEO site that sells plumbing leads."

It is:

> **A decision engine that determines what type of water heater makes sense, explains why, then connects the homeowner with the right type of local installer.**

Your requested quiz-first approach is particularly important. The brief explicitly calls for giving the homeowner useful recommendations before asking for contact information.

### **The long-term moat**

SEO content alone is weak.

The moat becomes:

**Decision logic \+ local data \+ installer capability data \+ homeowner outcome data.**

Eventually Water Heater Advisor could know things like:

* Which technology is usually selected by households with certain conditions.  
* Which Modesto installers actually handle heat-pump conversions.  
* Which contractors close Navien replacement leads.  
* Typical upgrade costs for different home configurations.  
* Which recommendations homeowners reject and why.  
* Actual quoted and sold prices by project type.  
* How often an "I want tankless" homeowner ends up choosing something else.

That data becomes much harder for a generic publisher or plumbing company to duplicate.

# **3\. Homeowner decision journey**

There are really **two funnels**.

### **Emergency funnel**

Failed heater → "Can this be repaired?" → "How fast can I get hot water?" → replacement choice → installer availability → price → job.

The homeowner's dominant concerns are:

* Is it dangerous?  
* Can it be repaired?  
* Can someone come today?  
* What is this going to cost?  
* Do I have time to consider tankless?  
* Am I getting upsold because I'm desperate?

Technology research becomes secondary.

### **Planned-upgrade funnel**

Aging heater / remodel / high bill / tankless interest / electrification → research technologies → sizing → cost → operating cost → brands → rebates → contractor → quotes → installation.

The dominant concerns become:

* Is tankless really better?  
* What would work for my household?  
* Will I actually save money?  
* How expensive is the conversion?  
* Does my electrical panel or gas line need work?  
* Which brand is reliable?  
* Who services that brand locally?  
* Are rebates still available?  
* Am I going to regret switching technologies?

# **4\. Water-heater recommendation framework**

The site should never start with "Which tankless heater should I buy?"

Start with:

**"What water-heating system makes the most sense for this home?"**

| Technology | Strong fit | Poor fit / caution |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Gas storage | Failed gas tank, urgent replacement, lower upfront budget, simple like-for-like job | Homeowner wants electrification or highest efficiency |
| Electric storage | Existing electric tank, low installation complexity | Higher operating efficiency is a major priority |
| Gas tankless | High continuous demand, limited space, gas available, homeowner values endless hot water | Expensive gas/vent conversion, poor maintenance habits, low hot-water use |
| Electric tankless | Small or specialized applications, adequate electric capacity | Many whole-home retrofits because electrical demand can be substantial |
| Heat-pump water heater | Warm garage/utility location, efficiency priority, electric conversion, solar, high water use | Very confined location, difficult condensate route, insufficient electrical capacity, noise-sensitive location |
| High-efficiency/condensing storage | Homeowner wants storage characteristics plus greater gas efficiency | Higher equipment/installation cost may outweigh benefit |
| 120V HPWH | Some retrofit situations where new 240V work is difficult | Recovery and household-demand constraints must be checked |

ENERGY STAR specifically recommends considering space, airflow, noise, condensate, electrical service and hot-water demand for HPWHs. It also recommends upsizing HPWH storage in high-demand households because a larger tank reduces use of the less-efficient resistance backup elements.

For tankless units, sizing should use **maximum simultaneous flow plus required temperature rise**, not household size alone. Rinnai's own sizing methodology takes simultaneous fixtures and incoming groundwater temperature into account.

### **The quiz should sometimes say:**

**"Based on your answers, we would not recommend converting to tankless."**

That may become one of the site's strongest trust signals.

**Emergency →**  
 shorten the funnel.

# **7\. Why Modesto is a good MVP market**

Modesto has roughly **72,989 households**, 58.9% owner occupancy and 2.96 people per household. That is enough housing density to test the concept without starting in an enormous metro.

### **Utility economics are genuinely local**

Modesto Irrigation District serves the greater Modesto electric territory. That alone is important because California water-heater economics cannot safely be modeled with a generic "California electricity rate." Utility territory must become a database field.

Nearby Turlock illustrates the problem. Turlock Irrigation District currently lists a **$350 HPWH rebate** for qualifying replacements of electric storage heaters.

A homeowner a short drive away may therefore get a different recommendation or payback calculation.

### **Permit information is useful content**

The City of Modesto lets homeowners apply for, pay for and obtain water-heater permits online. Properly licensed contractors can do the same, and inspections can be scheduled online.

That is exactly the type of local information generic national guides tend to omit.

### **California rules now matter more**

California's 2025 Energy Code applies to permit applications filed on or after January 1, 2026\. For newly constructed single-family homes using gas or propane water heating, the code includes HPWH-ready requirements such as designated space, electrical infrastructure and condensate provisions.

Modesto also adopted the 2025 California Mechanical Code with a June 11, 2026 effective date.

This gives Water Heater Advisor another valuable role: telling the homeowner which rules actually apply to **their** project instead of publishing vague "California requires..." statements.

### **Current rebate information is especially messy**

This is an opportunity.

The federal 25C heat-pump water-heater credit ended for property placed in service after **December 31, 2025**. Many articles written during the IRA period still reference the old 30%, up-to-$2,000 credit.

California's HEEHRA single-family rebates are also currently fully reserved statewide, with new projects going onto waitlists.

A homeowner-facing page that clearly says:

**ACTIVE / RESERVED / EXPIRED / VERIFY**

would be much more useful than another generic rebate article.

### **Water hardness**

I found evidence suggesting hard-water conditions in parts of Modesto, but not an authoritative enough source to publish one citywide hardness number.

So I would **not** write:

> "Modesto has 12.8 GPG water."

yet.

Instead:

> "Water hardness varies by source and location. Check your ZIP/water provider."

Then collect authoritative water-quality data by service zone.

That is a good example of the editorial standard Water Heater Advisor should use everywhere.

# **4\. Water-heater recommendation framework**

The starting question should remain:

> **What kind of water-heating system makes the most sense for this home?**

Not:

> Which tankless heater should I buy?

### **Technology logic**

| Technology | Stronger fit | Major caution |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Gas storage | Existing gas tank, emergency, simple replacement, upfront budget | Lower efficiency goals/electrification |
| Electric storage | Existing electric installation, simplicity | Operating efficiency |
| Gas tankless | Gas available, continuous demand, space concerns, high hot-water use | Gas-line/venting conversion, maintenance |
| Electric tankless | Small/point-of-use applications, sufficient electrical service | Whole-home electrical demand can be substantial |
| HPWH | Garage/utility area, efficiency, electrification, solar, adequate space | Air volume, condensate, electrical capacity, noise/location |
| High-efficiency gas storage | Wants tank behavior with better efficiency | Equipment/install economics |
| 120V HPWH | Selected retrofit cases where 240V work is difficult | Recovery and household demand must be carefully checked |

For HPWHs, ENERGY STAR specifically calls out installation volume, airflow, condensate, noise and tank size. Typical units may require roughly 450 to 700 cubic feet of surrounding air unless appropriate ventilation or ducting is provided.

Tankless sizing should use **simultaneous GPM demand plus required temperature rise**, not "four people \= this size." Rinnai itself uses flow rate, groundwater temperature and required temperature rise in its sizing approach.

This is where Water Heater Advisor can earn trust.

Sometimes the answer needs to be:

> **Based on your home and priorities, converting to tankless probably does not make sense.**

The original research identified this as an important trust signal.

**Control:** no local page without meaningful local data.

### **2\. Giving technically bad recommendations**

**Control:** rule-based feasibility first. Qualified professional validation where necessary. Do not let AI freely invent sizing or installation requirements.

**Control:** every volatile data record gets source, check date, expiration/status and review schedule.

### **4\. Monetization corrupts recommendations**

This is probably the most important strategic risk.

**Control:** contractor payment can affect **which eligible contractor receives the lead**, but never which technology Water Heater Advisor recommends.

That principle should become policy.

### **5\. Bad contractor experience**

**Control:** license verification, buyer screening, service standards, complaint tracking and homeowner follow-up.

### **6\. Manufacturer-status claims**

**Control:** never call someone "authorized," "certified" or equivalent unless the relevant manufacturer source supports that exact status.

### **7\. California contractor-referral law**

This needs to shape the business from day one.

CSLB says referral services are legal within limits, but an unlicensed referral company cannot present itself as performing the construction, solicit or negotiate home-improvement contracts on the contractor's behalf, or act as though it can undertake the construction itself. CSLB also says the homeowner should contract with and pay the licensed contractor directly.

That supports your existing positioning:

> **Water Heater Advisor is not a plumbing company.**

That language is already explicit in the original brief.

Have California counsel review the exact marketplace structure, contractor agreements, advertising language and handoff flow before launch.

### **8\. Consumer data and privacy**

The California privacy rules become more important as this grows. The CCPA currently includes thresholds involving adjusted gross revenue, handling personal information of 100,000 or more consumers/households, or deriving 50% or more of annual revenue from selling/sharing personal information.

Even before Water Heater Advisor reaches statutory thresholds, design around transparent consent and limited data collection.

Also have counsel review call/text consent, data-sharing disclosures and lead-transfer language. I would not improvise those terms.

### **9\. SEO dependence**

**Control:** quiz/tool usage, email nurture, partnerships, manufacturer relationships, paid search and first-party data.

### **10\. AI content at scale**

**Control:** research templates, source dates, human/local enrichment and factual QA.
