# **Water Heater Advisor Deep Research: Turlock, California**

**Research date: August 20, 2026**

## **Executive conclusion**

**Recommendation: HIGH PRIORITY. Make Turlock the second Water Heater Advisor Hub.**

Turlock is a particularly useful second market because it tests the core Water Heater Advisor thesis under conditions that differ from Modesto in ways that can actually change a homeowner recommendation.

The strongest reason is **Turlock Irrigation District electricity**. TID's 2026 residential electricity rates range from **13.38¢ to 18.91¢ per kWh**, depending on season and usage tier. TID also currently offers a **$500 standard heat-pump water-heater rebate** and a **$1,000 gas-to-electric HPWH rebate**. Meanwhile, natural gas is supplied by PG\&E, whose January 2026 average bundled non-CARE residential rate was **$2.784 per therm**.

That creates exactly the kind of situation Water Heater Advisor is designed for:

> A homeowner cannot safely use a generic "California" answer to decide between gas tank, tankless, electric storage, and heat pump.

Turlock also has enough homeowners, enough single-family housing, enough water-heater contractors, and enough manufacturer/distributor coverage to support differentiated lead routing. It is close enough to Modesto to reuse some contractor relationships while giving us a genuine test of the **Hub \+ Satellite system** described in the expansion plan.

**Overall market score: approximately 8.8/10.**

The City identifies PG\&E as Turlock's natural-gas provider.

* CARE: **$2.205/therm**  
* Non-CARE: **$2.784/therm**

# **3\. Rebates and incentives**

This category changes quickly, so every entry needs a status and last-verified date.

## **Current status as of August 20, 2026**

| Program | Value | Status |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| TID ENERGY STAR HPWH | **$500** | **ACTIVE** |
| TID gas-to-electric HPWH | **$1,000** | **ACTIVE** |
| California HEEHRA single-family | Potentially substantial | **WAITLIST / FULLY RESERVED** |
| TECH Clean California single-family HPWH | Varies | **RESERVED / CLOSED TO NEW RESERVATIONS** |
| Federal 25C tax credit | Formerly applicable | **EXPIRED** |
| GoGreen Home financing | Financing, not simple rebate | **ACTIVE, VERIFY ELIGIBILITY** |

### **TID $500 HPWH rebate**

TID currently offers $500 for an ENERGY STAR HPWH. Its listed requirements include ENERGY STAR certification, an efficiency factor of at least 2.0, first-hour rating of at least 50 gallons, and installation outside conditioned living space, such as a garage.

### **TID $1,000 gas-to-electric HPWH rebate**

For a qualifying conversion from natural gas or propane to HPWH, TID currently offers **$1,000**.

The contractor invoice must document removal or capping of the gas/propane connection and installation of the electric connection.

TID says rebate applications generally must be submitted within six months of purchase, funds are first-come, first-served, and programs can change without notice.

### **HEEHRA**

California's single-family HEEHRA rebates are currently **fully reserved statewide**. Northern California projects that were not approved are being waitlisted in case funding becomes available.

This means Water Heater Advisor should **not tell a Turlock homeowner that HEEHRA money is currently available.**

### **TECH Clean California**

The standard single-family HPWH incentive program is also fully reserved and not accepting new reservations.

### **Federal tax credit**

The Section 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit no longer applies to property placed in service after December 31, 2025\.

This matters because many older water-heater articles still describe the former federal credit.

### **GoGreen Home**

California's GoGreen program still provides financing support for energy upgrades, including heat-pump water heaters. Certain low-income households in qualifying communities may receive heavily reduced interest rates. TID customers can qualify because the program was expanded to customers of publicly owned utilities.

### **Water Heater Advisor opportunity**

A Turlock rebate page should display:

**ACTIVE**  
 TID HPWH: $500

**ACTIVE**  
 TID Gas → HPWH: $1,000

**WAITLIST**  
 HEEHRA

**RESERVED**  
 TECH Clean California

**EXPIRED**  
 Federal 25C

That page alone would be more useful than many existing "2026 water heater rebate" articles.

# **4\. Permits and building requirements**

This is unusually clear in Turlock.

The City explicitly states:

**Water heater replacement requires a building permit.**

The city's website provides online permit access and Building & Safety resources. I did **not** find a reliable fixed current water-heater permit fee, so Water Heater Advisor should not publish one yet.

Turlock currently lists the following enacted codes:

* 2025 California Residential Code  
* 2025 California Electrical Code  
* 2025 California Mechanical Code  
* 2025 California Plumbing Code  
* 2025 California Energy Code  
* other applicable 2025 California codes

The City also identifies Turlock as **Climate Zone 12**.

### **Recommendation**

The Turlock local page should have a simple section:

**Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Turlock?**

Answer: **Yes.**

Then explain that conversions can involve additional electrical, plumbing, gas, venting, or mechanical work that also has to meet current code.

We should avoid turning the website into a substitute for the Building Department or providing project-specific code interpretations.

# **5\. Turlock water conditions**

This produced one of the most useful local discoveries.

Turlock does **not** receive water from one uniform source.

The city uses:

* local groundwater  
* surface water purchased from the Stanislaus Regional Water Authority  
* surface water originating from the Tuolumne River

The city system includes **15 active wells**, three pump stations and four storage tanks.

## **Hardness**

The 2025 water-quality report lists groundwater total hardness as:

**89 ppm average**

with a range of:

**26.2 to 154 ppm as CaCO3**

The SRWA surface-water column reported no detected total hardness in that table. Groundwater TDS was 237 ppm compared with 35 ppm for the SRWA supply.

The city itself warns that hardness varies with geology and groundwater conditions and that hard water can cause scale in water heaters, pipes and appliances.

## **This changes our earlier local-page model**

We should **not publish:**

> "Turlock water hardness is 89 ppm."

That would imply every homeowner receives the same water.

A better Water Heater Advisor answer is:

> Turlock uses a mix of groundwater and Tuolumne River surface water. Groundwater hardness measured from 26.2 to 154 ppm in the city's 2025 testing, so scale conditions can vary.

Then ask:

**What ZIP/address or water service area are you in?**

This becomes especially useful for tankless owners because scale affects flushing and maintenance.

# **6\. Climate and physical conditions**

Turlock is officially in California **Climate Zone 12**.

TID describes its territory as having hot, dry summers.

That generally strengthens the HPWH case when a homeowner has a suitable garage or utility space.

ENERGY STAR says integrated HPWHs typically operate in spaces around 40°F to 120°F and become more efficient with warmer surrounding air. Garages can be particularly good locations in warmer climates. Air volume, condensate drainage, noise and manufacturer installation requirements still matter.

### **Recommendation logic**

A Turlock homeowner with:

* garage installation  
* suitable electrical capacity  
* adequate airflow  
* usable condensate route  
* moderate/high hot-water demand

could be an especially strong HPWH candidate.

But the quiz should not automatically recommend one.

For example, an emergency gas-tank replacement where the homeowner needs hot water restored today may still favor a simple gas tank replacement.
