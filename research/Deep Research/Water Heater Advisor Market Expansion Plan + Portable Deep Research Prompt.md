# Water Heater Advisor Market Expansion Plan

## Purpose

Water Heater Advisor is a nationwide homeowner education and local lead-generation platform.

It is **not a plumbing company** and should never present itself as one. Its role is to help homeowners:

- Understand their water-heating options
- Compare technologies and brands
- Decide what fits their home
- Understand likely installation issues and costs
- Find relevant rebates and incentives
- Connect with qualified local installers

The business model depends on generating **highly qualified homeowner leads** and routing each lead to the most appropriate local partner based on technology, brand, urgency, project type, geography, and installer capability.

---

# 1. Geographic Expansion Model

Use a **Hub + Satellite** strategy.

Do not perform the same level of research for every city.

## Hub Markets

A Hub Market gets a full Deep Research project.

Examples:

- Modesto
- Turlock
- Manteca
- Stockton
- Tracy
- Merced
- Lodi

A Hub should have enough population, homeowners, contractors, search demand, and economic differences to justify its own detailed market research.

Full Hub research includes:

- Utility providers
- Electricity and gas economics
- Rebates
- Permits
- Local building rules
- Housing characteristics
- Water conditions
- Contractor ecosystem
- Manufacturer and distributor presence
- Local pricing
- Lead buyers
- Competitive search environment
- Local content opportunities

---

## Satellite Markets

Satellite cities receive a smaller research pass.

Examples around Modesto could include:

- Ceres
- Riverbank
- Salida
- Escalon
- Ripon
- Hughson
- Keyes
- Denair
- Waterford

They still get genuine local pages, but they inherit information from:

1. National Water Heater Advisor research
2. California research
3. Their nearest Hub Market

Only facts that can materially differ by city need fresh research.

Examples:

- Electric utility
- Gas utility
- Rebates
- Permit authority
- Local permit requirements
- Water provider
- Water hardness or quality
- Local housing characteristics
- Installer availability
- Local service areas
- Any meaningful local pricing differences

This avoids creating thin city-name-swap pages while also avoiding unnecessary research.

The original research already established that national content should cover technology, sizing principles, brands, maintenance, and general installation issues, while utilities, rates, rebates, permits, codes, local prices, water conditions, and installer coverage should be localized.

# 6. Hub Research vs Satellite Research

## Hub Research

Full Deep Research.

Expected research depth:

**100%**

Investigate the entire market.

---

## Satellite Research

Expected research depth:

**20% to 35% of a Hub**

Focus only on facts that may differ.

Satellite research should answer:

1. Is the utility different?
2. Are rebates different?
3. Is the permit authority different?
4. Is the water provider different?
5. Are housing conditions meaningfully different?
6. Do the Hub installers serve this city?
7. Are there important local contractors not present in the Hub?
8. Is there any meaningful local pricing difference?
9. Is there enough unique information to justify a standalone local page?

If not, the page should not be created simply for SEO.

---

# 7. Expansion Decision Rule

Do not prioritize cities based solely on population or keyword volume.

Prioritize markets according to:

### 1. Installer opportunity
Can we find strong potential lead buyers?

### 2. Homeowner opportunity
Are there enough owner-occupied households and replacement opportunities?

### 3. Localization opportunity
Do utility, rebate, housing, water, permitting, or contractor differences create genuinely useful local information?

### 4. Market adjacency
Can existing buyers serve the new city?

### 5. Research leverage
Can much of the existing market research be reused?

### 6. Competitive opportunity
Can Water Heater Advisor provide something materially better than contractor pages, generic SEO articles, lead marketplaces, and manufacturer content?

### 7. Operational simplicity
Can we enter this market without creating a large new contractor-management burden?
